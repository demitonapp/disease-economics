A report for the Treasury's Infrastructure Transactions Unit on why public-sector construction contracts built on NZS 3910 and 3916 are not working for contractors or clients. It rests on consultation with industry and public-sector parties and a literature review; it does not report a sample of projects or a count of claims.

On variations and extensions of time it records an industry complaint that special conditions impose time bars, often 10 working days, after which a contractor is not paid for a variation it has already carried out. It names this a "culture of contractor disentitlement" (PDF p.8) and gives the public sector's side: cost and time certainty. It states no dollar value, no share of claims time-barred and no count. On ground risk it says the most common risk transfer "by stealth" is geotechnical reports issued with no reliance clause, so the contractor carries the ground risk without being able to rely on the report, and that this carries a cost; again no amount. It cites a New Zealand Institute of Quantity Surveyors survey (2018) in which 92% of respondents saw a risk allocation problem; that survey is quoted second-hand and was not opened.
