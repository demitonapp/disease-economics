An economist's 12-page report commissioned by the national body for civil contractors, written six weeks after
the February 2026 oil price shock. It reports that terminal gate diesel rose 145.2 cents a litre (88.9%) by the end
of March 2026, and that the 1,150 CCF member firms use about 88 million litres of diesel a month, so the rise costs
civil businesses in the order of $128 million a month, or $1.53 billion if sustained for 12 months. It puts that
annual figure at 1.2% of the 2025 value of engineering construction work done by the private sector. It
also says the CCF estimates 80 to 95% of civil contracts (by number) are fixed price, and that prices of bitumen,
explosives, plastic pipe and similar oil-linked products have risen 35% to 50% by anecdotal evidence. The
usage figures come from a CCF member survey (Chart 10); the commissioning body supplied the margin and
fixed-price estimates.
