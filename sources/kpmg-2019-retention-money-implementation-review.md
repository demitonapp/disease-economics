A review MBIE commissioned of how the 2017 retention money regime was working, from a survey of 223 construction firms (71 responded) and interviews with industry, banks, insolvency practitioners and others. It found most firms complying.

Its money is case by case, not a rate: at the Ebert receivership NZ$4,466,071 of post-regime retentions had been withheld, of which subcontractors holding NZ$856,141 had no interest in the trust account, and a further NZ$4.858 million of pre-regime retentions and NZ$24.52 million of unsecured trade creditor claims stood outside it. In four insolvencies about NZ$8.35 million sat in retention accounts for subcontractors. It also says the regime gives no protection against the larger loss in an insolvency, the one to two months of unpaid claims and work in progress, which is almost always not recoverable, and gives no amount for it.
