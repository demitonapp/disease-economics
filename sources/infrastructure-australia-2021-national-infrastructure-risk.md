The national market-capacity study built on a market sounding survey of government and contractor executives. On ground conditions it reports who respondents think should carry the risk: government and private executives broadly agree that latent ground conditions should be shared (44% and 38%), and 8% of government executives would put it wholly on the private sector. It says contractors are less willing to accept ground risk and that no-reliance clauses on tender geotechnical reports leave them without time or cost relief. It cites the Sydney CBD Light Rail utilities dispute as an overrun of the order of $1 billion, about 50% of project value. It gives no measure of how often latent-condition claims are made, rejected or time-barred, and no cost of ground risk as a share of contract value.
