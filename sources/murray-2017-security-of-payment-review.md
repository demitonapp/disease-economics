A national review of the eight state and territory security of payment Acts, commissioned by the Australian
Government. It draws on written submissions, consultation meetings and a survey of contractors run by AMR
Interactive, which industry associations circulated to their members on a voluntary basis.

Appendix B reports 526 survey responses from all states and territories (46.8% Queensland, 30.8% Western
Australia, 17.9% NSW; 48% subcontractors; 45% electrical). Of respondents, 23% had ever made a claim for delayed
or disputed payment under the Acts, and the average dollar amount of their most recent such claim was $221,633.
The most common band, chosen by 25% of claimants who had claimed in the last two years, was $100,001 to $500,000.

Elsewhere the report says there are around 2,000 adjudication applications a year in Australia (p.64), that
stakeholders regard disputed variations, time-related costs and latent conditions as too complex for rapid
adjudication (p.123), and that head contractors raise defect and liquidated damages issues late in a job so as to
hold retention money (p.270). None of those passages gives an amount.
