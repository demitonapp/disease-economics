Commissioned by the industry body that runs the national one-call service, and modelled on a 2019 University of Birmingham study of UK utility strikes. It takes 16 utility-supplied case studies (four each in gas, energy, water and telecommunications), finds the direct repair cost of each, then applies the ratio of total economic cost to direct cost. About 15,276 strikes a year are reported by the 26 largest network operators. For the national total it uses a direct repair cost of $9,000 per strike that BYDA advised as conservative, against a case-study mean of $26,732, giving $137.5 million a year in direct repair costs. The report puts the cost to the economy and community at $4.6 billion a year, about 32.4 times the direct repair cost. The direct repair cost is borne by the utility unless it can be recovered from the party that caused the damage.
