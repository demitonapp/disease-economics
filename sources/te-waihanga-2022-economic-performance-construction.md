Estimates built on Statistics New Zealand data. Between 2000 and 2020 labour productivity (output per hour
worked) rose 23% in building construction, 25% in construction services and 5% in heavy and civil
engineering, against 30% for the measured economy. A model (a reduced-form Baumol equation) estimates
that matching building construction would have cut civil construction prices by about 10%, raised the
quantity and quality of civil output by 5% and cut labour requirements by 11%.

Productivity is measured as an index of output per hour across the industry, not as hours spent against
hours needed on a job. The report also shows median firm profit margins for construction firms (net 2.8%
to 4.0% over 2017 to 2021) and notes that annual paid hours per worker fell about 6% over 2000 to 2020.
