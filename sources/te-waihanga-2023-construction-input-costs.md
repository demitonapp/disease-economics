A Crown entity's analysis of what moves construction wages and material prices in New Zealand. Table 2
splits the inputs of the heavy and civil construction industry using Statistics New Zealand's 2020
input-output tables: direct materials 17.6% (of which diesel 1.7%, sand, gravel, clay, stone and bitumen
3.4%, structural metal 3.7%), direct equipment 10.1% (consumption of capital 3.8%, maintenance 2.1%,
equipment hire 1.7%, other 2.5%), intermediate service inputs 41.8% (civil engineering services 16.8%,
other installation work 13.5%, other services 11.4%) and taxes, subsidies and margins 8.0%. The labour
row of the printed table is blank; the text on p.10 puts direct labour at "around 22%" of infrastructure
construction costs.

The paper also reports average annual output price growth for heavy and civil engineering construction
(Table 1: 2.4% a year 2009 to 2019, 7.8% a year 2020 to the March 2023 quarter). Its Figure 1 puts the mean quarterly
change over 2010 to 2022 at 1.18% for materials and 0.90% for construction hourly wages, nominal.
