A consultancy report for the Department of Building and Housing (the copy read is hosted by interest.co.nz) that re-estimates the number and cost of weathertightness failures in New Zealand dwellings built 1992 to 2008. It combines a national risk profile built from building consents with failure counts from the Weathertight Homes Resolution Service, then reconciles them with expert opinion. The consensus forecast is 42,000 failing dwellings (range 22,000 to 89,000), with a total economic cost, repair plus transaction costs, of $11.3 billion in 2008 dollars ($11,284 million in Figure 26; $5,925 million on the lower, claims-extrapolation view of about 22,000 failures). Under policy at the time, 69% of the cost falls on owners, 25% on councils, 4% on third parties including builders, and 2% on government. Figure 54 sets per-dwelling transaction costs (design, legal, experts, consequential) at $110,000 for a full reclad and $50,000 for targeted repair, in 2008 dollars. The report does not split failures between design, workmanship and materials.
