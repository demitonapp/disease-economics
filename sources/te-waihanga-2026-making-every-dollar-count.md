Te Waihanga's review of why infrastructure costs what it does in New Zealand. It names three drivers (input
costs, construction productivity, project scope and design) and reports that civil labour productivity rose
7% from 2000 to 2020 against 21% for building construction (its 2022 report gave 5% and 23% for the same
years; this edition's figures differ).

Figures 32 and 33 use the National Infrastructure Pipeline's reported cost changes. From financial decision to close, 2,486
projects: high-value initiatives (over $100 million, 43 projects) reported a cost increase 51% of the time
(44% up to 50%, 7% over 50%), all others (2,443) 28% of the time (18% and 10%). These are shares of projects
with a change, not sizes of cost change.

Material prices are said to follow world commodity prices closely: a 1% rise in the world price is estimated
to raise the New Zealand price of steel, diesel and timber by about 0.6% (p.17), and in the 53 quarters from
2010 to 2022 quarterly price movements above 2% occurred in almost half of all quarters (p.17).
