A benchmarking report built on Oxford Global Projects data comparing the delivery cost of eight infrastructure types with other high-income countries. It finds a cost premium on complex large projects (motorways, road tunnels, underground rail) and credits input costs, productivity and the physical environment.

It says tunnelling in difficult ground can cost up to three times as much as in favourable ground and that "further work is needed to quantify" how far New Zealand's geology raises costs. Its passage on variations summarises a California road study, not New Zealand data. It holds no measure of claims missed or time-barred, and no liquidated damages or delay cost figure.
